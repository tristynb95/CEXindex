Coffee Experience Index v4 — Methodology

How Do We Measure Success?

The Coffee Experience Index uses a 0 to 100 score to tell us how well we're doing. We look at this in two ways: Relative Score (how you compare to other bakeries this selected period) and Absolute Score (how you compare to GAIL's overall strict standards, regardless of everyone else).

Relative vs Absolute Explainer

Relative Score is like a race. Every selected period, all bakeries line up and we rank them against each other. If you finish 1st out of 20 bakeries, your score is near 100. If you finish last, it's near 0. It doesn't matter what the actual numbers are — only where you finished in the group that selected period.

Absolute Score is like a test with a pass mark. The pass mark is 90%. Whether every other bakery is above or below 90% doesn't change your result — you either hit the standard or you didn't.

Use Relative to see who's leading the pack. Use Absolute to see if the whole network is genuinely hitting the standard.

The Recipe: What Makes Up the Score?

We believe what the customer tells us matters most. That's why 95% of your score comes directly from customer feedback:

  • Efficiency (35%) — Did the customer feel they were served quickly?
  • Drink Quality (35%) — Did the customer love their coffee?
  • Friendliness (25%) — Did the customer feel welcome?
  • Coffee Efficiency (5%) — How fast the order was completed. We display the % under 2 mins (Target: 80% < 2 min) in the KPI cards and tables, but the index score uses a progressive 5-tier scale: 1.0x for under 2 mins, 0.5x for 2–3 mins, 0.25x for 3–4 mins, 0.1x for 4–5 mins, and a -5.0x penalty for orders over 5 mins.

Keeping It Fair

If a bakery only gets 2 or 3 reviews in a selected period, one bad review could unfairly ruin their score, or one perfect review could push them to the very top. To be fair, if you don't have many reviews, we gently blend your score with the average to keep things balanced and fair for everyone.

Our Standards (Gap-Based Penalties)

Our standards are built on hitting specific benchmarks: 90% or higher for customer feedback (Drink Quality, Efficiency, Friendliness), and 80% or higher for Coffee Efficiency. If a bakery meets or exceeds its benchmark, it receives full marks (100 points).

However, if a score drops below the benchmark, we apply progressive point deductions based on the gap from the benchmark, rather than absolute values:

  • 0% to 5% gap: Deducts 4 points for every 1% below benchmark (up to 20 points lost).
  • 5% to 15% gap: Deducts 6 points for every 1% below benchmark (up to an additional 60 points lost).
  • 15% to 20% gap: Deducts 4 points for every 1% below benchmark, reaching 0 points at a 20% gap.

Example (90% Benchmark): Hitting 90% gives 100. A score of 80% (10% gap) loses half its points (50). Any score of 70% or lower (20%+ gap) gets 0.

KPI Card Status Thresholds

To help scan the dashboard quickly, each KPI card displays a color-coded status tag (Exceeding, On Target, Watch, or Below) based on specific performance bands for the selected period:

  • Net Promoter Score (NPS): Exceeding (>60), On Target (55–60), Watch (45–54), Below (<45).
  • SHINE Cards (Drink Quality, Efficiency, Friendliness): Exceeding (≥93%), On Target (90–92%), Watch (80–89%), Below (<80%).
  • Coffee Efficiency: Exceeding (>80%), On Target (70–80%), Watch (60–69%), Below (<60%).
  • Orders >5 Min: Exceeding (<0.5%), On Target (0.5–0.9%), Watch (1.0–1.4%), Below (≥1.5%).

What's New in v4?

1. Listening to the customer: We reduced the score for Coffee Efficiency from 20% down to 5%, and instead put that focus strictly on how the customer rated their speed and quality.

2. Fairer rankings for everyone: We added the fairness adjustment for bakeries with very few reviews, so rankings aren't thrown off by just a few responses.

3. A true standard to aim for: We added the Absolute Score to make sure we are not just trying to beat each other week-over-week, but genuinely improving our service and meeting our company targets.
