How the score is calculated

The headline result is the Benchmark Score: a number from 0 to 100 showing how closely a bakery met six fixed company standards. Each standard earns points out of 100, and the points are weighted into one score. Other bakeries do not change the raw result. Response volume can change the published result through the low-volume adjustment.

Unit One bakery, one selected period

Monthly source results are combined when more than one month is selected.

Main result Adjusted Benchmark Score

Six component scores are weighted, then adjusted only where response volume is low.

Precision Calculated to one decimal place

The headline card rounds the result to a whole number for display.

Selection summaries: when several bakeries are selected, the dashboard card shows their arithmetic mean. Each bakery is scored first; the source data is not pooled into one combined bakery.

    Audit trail

    The rules behind the number

    Everything an auditor needs to reproduce a published result, kept out of the way until it is needed.

    Low response volume adjustment

    Low response counts make a score more sensitive to one response. To reduce that effect, the raw score is blended with the mean raw score of all scored bakeries in the same company cohort.

    published score = (raw score × bakery share) + (cohort mean × cohort share)
    Shares used in the response-volume adjustment
    Responses Historic or multi-month period Single current month, normal volume Single current month, late low-volume case
    15 or more 100% raw 100% raw 100% raw
    8–14 75% raw + 25% cohort mean 100% raw 85% raw + 15% cohort mean
    1–7 35% raw + 65% cohort mean 100% raw 55% raw + 45% cohort mean
    0 Not scored

    A current-month record is a late low-volume case only on or after the later of day 21 and seven days before month-end, and only when its response count is below half of the cohort median. The calculator above uses the historic / multi-month column.

    Periods, missing data and ranking

    More than one month selected

    Each KPI is the simple arithmetic mean of the bakery's scored monthly values; it is not weighted by monthly response volume, and months with no scored data are left out of the average rather than counted as zero. Response volume and drink counts are summed over the same scored months. A bakery is scored for the period once it has three scored months and is still reporting — at least one of those months falls in the final three months present in the period. Below that floor, or where the data stops before the period closes, the result is marked incomplete and is not ranked. Periods of three months or fewer therefore still require every month present.

    Missing source values

    Zero responses means no score. Blank core rating or timing fields are imported as 0. A blank average wait remains missing; the current calculation gives it 100 benchmark component points and the neutral 50th percentile in the peer calculation. Auditors should therefore check blank wait fields before relying on a result.

    Company rank

    Eligible bakeries are ordered by adjusted Benchmark Score, highest first. An adjusted peer score breaks a benchmark-score tie; bakery name breaks any remaining tie. Region, ops-area and bakery filters are applied after the company score and rank are fixed, so filtering does not change company rank.

    Published bands

    The adjusted Benchmark Score sets the band, shown alongside the calculator above. Decimal values are compared with the exact cut-offs before any whole-number display rounding.

    Peer tie-break calculation

    The peer score is not the headline performance measure. It is retained to compare relative position and to break ties in company rank.

    For a monthly result, each of the same six measures is converted to a percentile within the scored company cohort. The lowest result receives 0 and the highest receives 100; lower wait time is better. Ties use the average tied position. A one-bakery cohort receives 50. The six percentiles use the same 20%, 20%, 20%, 20%, 10% and 10% weights, followed by the same response-volume adjustment.

    For a multi-month result, the quality, customer-efficiency and friendliness peer components are averages of their monthly percentiles. NPS, within-2-minute performance and average-wait percentiles are recalculated from the period averages.

    KPI card labels

    Card labels help with scanning and do not add points to the index. They use these display thresholds:

    • Drink + Meal NPS: Exceeding above 60; On Target 55–60; Watch 45–<55; Below under 45.
    • Quality, customer-rated efficiency and friendliness: Exceeding above 92%; On Target 90–92%; Watch 80–<90%; Below under 80%.
    • Coffee efficiency headline: Exceeding above 80%; On Target 70–80%; Watch 60–<70%; Below under 60%.
    • Average wait: On Target at 120 seconds or less; Watch above 120 and below 125 seconds; Below at 125 seconds or more.
    • Orders over 5 minutes: Exceeding under 0.5%; On Target 0.5–1.0%; Watch above 1.0% and below 2.5%; Below at 2.5% or more.

    The Benchmark Score card uses a separate display label: Exceeding above 92, Meeting from 75 through 92, Approaching from 60 to under 75, and Below Standard under 60. The formal score bands used in tables, maps and rankings are the published bands shown above.

    How to reproduce a result
    1. Choose the same period and use the full, unfiltered company cohort.
    2. Confirm the bakery's six source inputs and response volume. For a multi-month period, confirm that every expected month is present.
    3. Enter those inputs in the calculator above, or convert them by hand using the targets and floors in section 01.
    4. Apply the six weights and round the raw score to one decimal place.
    5. Calculate the cohort mean of raw scores, apply the response-volume blend where required, and round the published score to one decimal place.
    6. Assign the score band and rank the complete cohort before applying dashboard filters.
